Cars Are Getting Bigger and More Dangerous

Cars are getting bigger and putting more people's lives at risk, according to a new report from the European Federation for Transport and Environment.

The report looked at new vehicles sold in Europe since the year 2000.It found that, each year, Europe's cars have been getting an average of 1.2 centimeters longer, 0.5 centimeters taller and 0.5 centimeters wider.

Previous research has shown that, in a crash, larger vehicles cause more serious injuries to vulnerable road users like pedestrians and cyclists.In particular, when the front of a car is higher off the ground, it's more of a danger for pedestrians, because it might hit their chest or head.

The new report points to previous studies that found a 10 centimeter increase in height at the front of a car increases the risk of causing death to vulnerable road users by 27%.And the same increase in height increases the risk of a child dying by 81%.

Based on this previous research, the authors say that if cars in Europe continue to get bigger at the same rate, by 2040 they will cause a total of nearly 2,600 extra crash deaths — including 79 children.

Bigger cars also have other negative impacts.

The report says that if the trend for bigger cars continues, by 2040 European cities will lose between 8.5% and 14% of their on-street parking spaces because fewer cars will be able to fit into existing spaces.

Bigger cars also need more energy.By 2040, they'll have made Europe import over 100 million extra barrels of oil, the report says.The continent will also need more power generation as electric vehicles get bigger, while their drivers will have spent an extra $41 billion charging those cars by 2040.

The authors suggest setting legal limits on the height and width of cars, taxing bigger cars to discourage people from buying them, and charging larger vehicles more for using parking spaces.
