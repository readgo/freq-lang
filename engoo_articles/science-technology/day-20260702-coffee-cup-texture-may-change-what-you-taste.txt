Coffee Cup Texture May Change What You Taste

Do you ever feel like your coffee somehow tastes better out of your favorite cup?It might be because of how the cup feels in your hands!

A study from Tokyo's Chuo University has looked at how the textures we feel in our hands while drinking coffee could affect how we perceive its taste.

Older studies have found that things like the color, shape and material of cups can change how people think about a drink's taste.However, it hasn't been clear if this change is the result of visual cues or touch.

So first the researchers made two types of cup sleeves — a rough one constructed from sandpaper and one made from smooth paper.

Then the researchers had 92 participants put on blindfolds before they were all given the same kind of black coffee, served at the same temperature.

Half of the participants were first served coffee in a cup with a smooth sleeve, and then given the exact same coffee in a cup with a rough sleeve.The other participants were first given coffee with a rough sleeve before getting it with a smooth sleeve.

After their second cup, participants were asked to rate the sweetness and acidity of what they had just drunk.

The participants who started with a rough sleeve were more likely to say their second, smooth-sleeved cup of coffee was less acidic than the first.However, participants who went from smooth to rough didn't report any change in how acidic the coffee tasted.

The researchers say this may be because our minds link rough textures with strong acidity, and smooth textures with mild acidity.Touching a smooth surface after a rough one can make this association stronger, making what we are drinking seem milder.

The researchers say their results could lead to new ways of designing and choosing cups — or at least sleeves — to better appeal to individual preferences.
