Video Games Could Reduce Loneliness, Says Study

Playing video games could be more than just fun — it could help build emotional resilience and reduce feelings of loneliness.

This is according to research published in _JMIR Serious Games_.It was based on a survey of more than 2,000 adults who were asked about their levels of loneliness, as well as questions to measure their stoicism.

Stoicism in this study meant emotional resilience, self-control and the ability to handle challenges calmly.While stoicism might not encourage someone to go out and meet new people, it can help them build a sense of self-worth from within.

This stronger sense of self-worth might reduce the negative feelings someone could have when they are by themselves.This means that they don't necessarily feel lonely, even when they are alone.

The researchers used the survey answers to compare participants who didn't play video games with those who had recently played one of four Nintendo games — _The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild_, _The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom_,  _Yoshi's Crafted World_ or  _Yoshi's Woolly World_.

The study found that people who played these games reported lower levels of loneliness than those who didn't.Game players also showed more signs of stoicism.

While playing games can't replace professional mental healthcare, the study's findings suggest that thoughtfully chosen game experiences could be a tool to support emotional well-being.

The two _Legend of Zelda_ games offer an open world that the player, as main character Link, starts off exploring with very few resources.The player has to work to find the resources they need to progress in the game, which teaches self-sufficiency — an important part of stoicism.

The _Yoshi_ games are far gentler, but the researchers say the gameplay and simple, creative design encourage mindfulness and being present in the moment.This might reduce the anxiety often linked with feeling lonely.
