Kyoto Scooters Stop Automatically in No-Ride Zones

Rentable electric scooters have become more and more popular in cities around the world as a quick and easy way to get around.However, they have also become a danger in busy pedestrian areas.

Now, the electric scooter sharing company Luup has developed a system using GPS that automatically stops scooters if they enter restricted areas.To begin with, the system will be used to stop people riding scooters on walking paths beside Kyoto's Kamo River, a parklike area used by walkers, joggers and people enjoying picnics.

Luup started renting out electric scooters in Kyoto in {{prn:2021|twenty twenty-one}} and now has more than {{prn:1,440|one thousand four hundred forty}} parking ports across the city.The scooters have become a popular way for both residents and tourists to get around, partly because buses in Kyoto are often crowded.Anyone aged 16 or older can ride an electric scooter without a driving licence.

But as more people have started using electric scooters across Japan, the number of accidents involving them has also increased.

Government data shows that there were 386 accidents involving electric scooters and electric bicycles in 2025, 48 more than the year before.Around 10% of those accidents involved drunk riders.

So to help keep Kyoto safe, Luup developed the new automatic stopping system with Kyoto Prefecture and the Kyoto Prefectural Police.

Using GPS technology, the scooters know when they've entered restricted areas.Riders first hear a voice warning from their scooter.Then the scooter slows down, before coming to a stop.

Luup says the system will only be used in pedestrianised areas — like the Kamo River area — because slowing a scooter automatically on roads shared with cars could be dangerous.
