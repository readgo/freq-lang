2 Million French Households Are Now Millionaires 

Research on wealth in France has found that just a little under 2 million households are now millionaires.This is around 6.5% of households in the country.

This result was published in a report by Observatoire des Inegalites, an organization that studies social inequality in France.

Using government data, the report estimated that around {{prn:1.95 million|one point nine five million}} households now have net assets valued above {{prn:1 million euros|one million euros}}, or around {{prn:$1.2 million|one point two million dollars}}.

However, only about {{prn:181,000|one hundred eighty-one thousand}} households pay France's property wealth tax, which is owed on property worth {{prn:1.3 million euros|one point three million euros}} — about {{prn:$1.5 million|one point five million dollars}} — or more.This suggests that many, if not most, of France's millionaires have their money in assets other than property, which lets them avoid the property wealth tax.

The report also found that there are {{prn:145|one hundred forty-five}} billionaires now living in France.This includes Bernard Arnault, CEO of luxury brand company LVMH, who often tops the list of the richest people in Europe.

LVMH's brands include Sephora, Louis Vuitton, Christian Dior and Dom Perignon.According to _Forbes_, Arnault and his family are worth around {{prn:$150 billion|one hundred fifty billion dollars}}.

The report also found that the wealth of the richest {{prn:0.1%|zero point one percent}} of households in France has quadrupled since {{prn:2003|two thousand three}}, while the wealth of the {{prn:500|five hundred}} richest families is now around six times higher

Meanwhile, data released by France's national statistics agency in July 2025 showed that more than one person in 10 lived below the poverty line.This means they earned less than {{prn:1,288 euros|one thousand two hundred eighty-eight euros}} per month — or around {{prn:$1,500|one thousand five hundred dollars}} — which is {{prn:60%|sixty percent}} of the country's median income.

The people most affected by poverty in France include single parents, large families and students — all of whom are strongly affected by rising living costs, according to France-focused newspaper _The Connexion_.
