Europe's Heat Wave Fuels Asian Air Con Boom

This year, Europe has been suffering through an early summer heat wave that scientists have said is the worst on record.Many countries have seen temperatures go above 40 degrees Celsius, with the World Health Organization reporting that the deaths of 1,300 people over the last full week of June may have been caused by the heat.

Scientists say that heat waves like this are becoming more common because of climate change.But in Europe, the problem is aggravated by a lack of air conditioning.Although air conditioning is common in parts of the world like Asia, in the homes, schools and workplaces of Europe it is still rare.

But as temperatures could continue to rise through the summer, people and companies across Europe are now hurrying to buy air conditioners.And this has created a boom in sales for Asian manufacturers of these products.

Japan's Mitsubishi Electric told _Reuters_ that it has seen a surge in demand for air conditioners, with sales particularly strong in France, Spain, the UK and Germany — all countries badly affected by the recent heat wave.

South Korea's Samsung Electronics also reported a 10% increase in sales of air conditioners in countries like Italy, Spain and France in the first half of this year.

However, because installing a new air conditioning system in older European buildings can be difficult and expensive, portable air conditioners have also become a popular option.

China's Midea told _Reuters_ that it has seen a rise in demand from Europe for its PortaSplit portable air conditioners, with even secondhand units selling for high prices when new models have sold out.In May this year, sales of PortaSplit air conditioners in France and Spain were 108% higher than in May 2025.

According to the International Energy Agency, total ownership of air conditioners in Europe is only 20%, so as temperatures continue to rise, demand for new air conditioners is likely to remain high.
