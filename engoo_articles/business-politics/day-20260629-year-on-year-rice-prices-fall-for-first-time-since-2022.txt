Year-on-Year Rice Prices Fall for First Time Since 2022

In May, the year-on-year price of rice in Japan fell for the first time in three-and-a-half years.The last time rice prices fell year-on-year was in November 2022.

That's based on retail price data from Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications.

The data shows that the average price of a {{prn:5 kilogram|five-kilogram}} bag of rice rose from {{prn:2,028 yen|two thousand twenty-eight yen}} in {{prn:November 2022|November twenty twenty-two}} to as high as {{prn:5,002 yen|five thousand two yen}} in {{prn:November 2025|November twenty twenty-five}}.That's a rise from about {{prn:$12.54|twelve point five four dollars}} to about {{prn:$30.94|thirty point nine four dollars}} — an increase of nearly {{prn:150%|one hundred fifty percent}}.

But rice prices have been falling since {{prn:December|December}} last year, and in {{prn:May|May}} they hit {{prn:4,449 yen|four thousand four hundred forty-nine yen}}, or about {{prn:$27.52|twenty-seven point five two dollars}}.That's {{prn:5%|five percent}} lower than the price in {{prn:May|May}} last year, which was {{prn:4,689 yen|four thousand six hundred eighty-nine yen}}, or about {{prn:$28.99|twenty-eight point nine nine dollars}}.

Japan's rice prices began rising very quickly in 2024, and rose even more in 2025.

This has been blamed on a number of things, including poor harvests after a very hot and dry summer in 2023, followed by warnings that there was a high danger of a large earthquake in August 2024, which caused many people to panic-buy extra rice.

The growing number of tourists  in the country have also been blamed for eating up more of the rice supply.

With prices rising, Japanese people have been eating less rice, with some choosing to eat cheaper foods like noodles.In the spring of this year, the amount of rice each Japanese person ate was at its lowest level since 2018, according to a survey from a rice industry group.

The government has been trying to find ways to reduce the price of rice, including releasing 590,000 metric tons of emergency stockpiles in 2025.
