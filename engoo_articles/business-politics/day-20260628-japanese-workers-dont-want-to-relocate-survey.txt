Japanese Workers Don't Want to Relocate — Survey

In Japan, companies and government departments often tell staff to relocate to another part of the country to work in a different office.For example, a worker might spend three years working in Tokyo and then be asked to move to a branch in Nagano.

This practice is known in Japanese as _tenkin_.It has been common in Japan for a long time, especially in large companies and government organizations.

Workers may feel they cannot refuse a transfer because this could affect their careers.However, not everyone wants to relocate.

According to a new survey by Japan's National Personnel Authority (NPA), more than half of government workers do not want to move to another part of the country for work.

The survey found that 16% of workers said they "absolutely don't want" to relocate for work, while 36.5% said they would prefer not to.

About 100,000 people took part in the survey.

The NPA said government departments should think carefully about whether relocations are really necessary.

If departments do need to relocate workers, the NPA said they should offer more financial support to make the moves easier.

It also said that workers should be given plenty of notice before a transfer so they have time to prepare.

Of course, these moves affect not only the workers themselves but also their families.

When male workers in Japan are asked to relocate, their wives are often forced to choose between letting the husband move away on his own, or moving with him and giving up their own job.

A 2024 study by Japanese researchers Kozue Sekijima and Mayuko Abe found that more women are choosing to keep their jobs and let their husbands relocate alone.

In this way, _tenkin_ may be at odds with the Japanese government's efforts to encourage more people to have children.
