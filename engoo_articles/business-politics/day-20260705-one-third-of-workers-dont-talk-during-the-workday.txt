One-Third of Workers Don't Talk During the Workday

Almost one-third of office workers in the US often don't talk to another colleague during a workday.

This is also true for 17% of workers in the UK, according to a new survey by a company called Kahoot!.

It surveyed around 4,000 office workers over the age of 25 in the US and UK.

The survey found that, while workers might send a lot of emails and messages, many often go a whole day without having a face-to-face conversation with a colleague — and many report feelings of loneliness.

Thirty-nine percent of US workers and 40% of those in the UK said they feel lonely at work.

For many, meetings have become the main point of contact with colleagues, with around half of both US and UK workers saying that meetings are the only time they talk to some coworkers.

At the same time, workers know how important it is to connect with each other.For example, nearly two-thirds of US workers and over half of UK workers said meetings are more effective when they spend some time talking to their colleagues or bosses beforehand.

Research has shown that people are likely to work better when they feel connected to their colleagues and their workplace, and most workers in both the US and UK said they want closer connections with their colleagues.

However, the survey also found that some workers don't want to get too close or share too much personal information.

Kahoot! said that companies need to create workplaces where connections "can develop naturally without feeling forced."
