Saving Money Is Easier with Goals — US Survey

Working Americans who set clear goals for their savings are more likely to regularly save money than those without goals, according to a new survey.

Finance company NerdWallet surveyed more than 2,000 US adults in March this year.Of these, over {{prn:1,100|one thousand one hundred}} had jobs.

The survey found that 66% of working Americans regularly save money each month.

However, 22% of working Americans said they weren't sure how much they saved each time they got paid.And 8% said they weren't saving anything at all!

That means almost a third of working Americans might need some advice when it comes to saving money!

According to NerdWallet, setting a goal is a simple way to save more money — and the survey results seem to back this up.

Although 62% of those who had not set a goal were still able to regularly save some of their income, the proportion of those with clear goals who were able to save regularly was 75%.

The most common savings goal, named by 45%, was an emergency fund — money that might be needed for something unexpected, like a medical situation or suddenly losing a job.

That was followed by vacations at 34%.And 26% said they were saving for a car — either buying one, renting one or just keeping one going!

When asked what makes saving difficult, the top answer was having to pay off debts, named by 27% of participants.This was followed by not having a high enough income at 26%, and essential expenses being too high at 24%.

However, NerdWallet's Kate Ashford said that even if you can't save as much as you want to, "saving a little is better than saving zero."

She recommended setting up an automatic transfer to a savings account once or twice a month.

"You can always increase it later — and we hope you do — but you won't be sad if you started now," she added.
