Most Young Adults Want a Job that Helps Others

Most teenagers and young adults in the US want a job that helps other people, according to a new survey.

However, many say that these jobs are difficult and don't pay enough.

Gallup spoke to almost 2,500 people in the US aged between 13 and 28 as part of the _Voices of Gen Z_ report.

The survey found that 79% of participants would like a job that focuses on helping others or that makes a positive impact on the lives of others.

This might include working as a nurse, a doctor, a teacher or a care worker.

The survey found that 56% of participants who are employed said they currently work in a job like this.

Participants were also asked what would stop them from trying to get a job that involves helping people.Almost half gave low pay as a reason, while 46% said they think such jobs are difficult or draining.

However, Gallup found that young people who have jobs that help others are more likely to feel that their own lives have purpose or meaning.

Studies have shown that helping others is good for our mental health and can help us feel more connected to other people.
